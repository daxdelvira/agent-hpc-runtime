## ⚠️ The 256 GB rows contain an inversion worth reading carefully

At the production allocation, **retaining both classes under LRU is WORSE than
retaining data alone** — 26.97% vs 31.77% at slots=1. Adding vLLM's sleep flag on
top of the persistent data worker *costs* about 5 points. That is why the Shapley
split credits vLLM with 2% (slots=1) and −1% (slots=2) at this budget.

It is not noise, and it is not a simulator defect. Verified directly
(mean ± sd over 3 orderings × 2 populations):

| policy | data-only | both classes | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| LRU (size- and cost-blind) | 31.58 ± 2.16 | 26.86 ± 1.31 | **−4.73** |
| cost-aware, 0.55 accuracy | 31.58 ± 2.16 | 32.87 ± 0.05 | **+1.29** |
| cost-aware, oracle | 31.58 ± 2.16 | 32.76 ± 0.45 | +1.18 |

**The mechanism.** At 256 GB the packing is `qwen_32b` (129.7 GB) + `uniref90`
(117.2 GB) = 246.9 GB, which fits, but adding `uniref50` (36.1 GB) reaches
283.0 GB, which does not. So the model and the data genuinely contend. LRU, being
blind to both size and cost, sometimes parks the 32B — cold 495.2 s but only 27
requests in the traces — in place of UniRef90, cold 372.6 s with 71 requests. It
trades a frequently-reused artifact for a rarely-reused one.

**This is the clearest case for the policy anywhere in these tables**, and it only
appears at a budget tight enough to force the trade. Note the asymmetry: the
cost-aware policy recovers the loss at slots=1 but not at slots=2, where a second
GPU slot already absorbs the model reuse and parking the 32B has little left to
offer.

**Consequence for how the ladder is read.** "vLLM contributes 33–61%" holds at
560 GB. At the allocation actually requested it contributes ~0, because neither
72B fits at all (279.0 and 276.3 GB against a 256 GB budget) and the only
parkable model is the least-used one. The vLLM share is therefore a function of
budget, not a property of the system, and should never be quoted without it.
