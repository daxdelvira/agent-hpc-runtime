\section{Conclusion}

Agentic steering breaks the phase predictability that byte-oriented staging
depends on, and the systems built on it fail structurally rather than for want
of accuracy. The unit of work is wrong as well: for these workloads most of
the cost of making data usable is a representation change performed inside a
live process, and that structure and a parked model's weights are the same
kind of object---anonymous memory drawn from one allocation. Treating them as
one budget rather than two tiers is what makes the residency decision
expressible at all.

\sysname{} makes that decision, ranking residency by stall avoided per
gigabyte held and bounding speculation to memory retention has not claimed. It
removes between a third and half of end-to-end wall time against a runtime
that retains nothing, and a further tenth to sixth against a baseline that
already retains both classes---on workflows where the byte-oriented
alternative makes wall time three times worse. As agentic steering reaches
workflows with larger artifacts and more models, the quantity worth scheduling
is not which bytes to move, but which structures to keep.
