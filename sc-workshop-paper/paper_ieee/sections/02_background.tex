\section{Background and Related Work}

\subsection{Traditional vs.\ Agentic Scientific Workflows}

An HPC workflow is a multi-stage application whose phases each correspond to a
computational task---a simulation, analysis, or visualization step---that
consumes data produced by earlier phases and generates data for later ones.
Phases are often cyclical, with the outputs of one series becoming the inputs
to the next. Workflows of this shape underpin protein and alloy discovery,
astronomical survey analysis, and computational fluid dynamics, and their use
has produced advances from new therapeutic proteins to the identification of
supernovae in telescope data streams.

\subsection{Prior Solutions for Memory Bottlenecks}

The datasets these workflows manipulate expose a growing imbalance between
compute throughput and memory bandwidth: over the past twenty years hardware
FLOPs have scaled $60000\times$, while DRAM bandwidth has scaled $100\times$
and interconnect bandwidth only $20\times$. A workflow whose working set
exceeds RAM becomes ``out-of-core'' for part or all of its execution, and the
resulting data movement is the memory wall in its practical form. Scaling DRAM
addresses it only at a financial and energy cost that does not generalize
across sites.

Recent work instead exposes tiered memory and storage through software
distributed shared memory, presenting the hierarchy as one large pool---a Deep
Memory and Storage Hierarchy (DMSH). Coherency and placement are made
affordable by the predictability of phase-based execution: a workflow declares
its intent and expected accesses through an API, and the system uses that
declaration to place data across tiers and to prefetch it during preceding
computation. That declaration is the assumption agentic workflows remove.

\subsection{Workload Description}

\paragraph{AtomAgents.}
AtomAgents is a multi-agent materials discovery framework built on AutoGen
that combines LLM reasoning with physics-based simulation tools such as
LAMMPS. Specialized agents collaborate to plan, execute, and refine scientific
workflows by invoking simulation tools through function-calling interfaces.
Each tool invocation represents a workflow phase whose required simulation
inputs, intermediate datasets, and even successor phases are determined only
after the agents complete their reasoning.
